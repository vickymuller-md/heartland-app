# HEARTLAND Protocol — One-Page Summary

**Heart Failure Evidence-based Access in Rural Treatment, Linking Advanced Network Delivery**

Published in *Cureus* (Springer Nature) | Author: Vicky Muller Ferreira, MD | ORCID: 0009-0009-1099-5690

---

## The Problem

- Heart failure affects millions of Americans; prevalence projected to nearly double by 2050
- Rural residents face higher HF incidence, higher mortality, and severely limited access to cardiologists (mean distance: 87 miles vs 16 miles in urban counties)
- Fewer than 1 in 4 eligible patients receive all recommended medication classes; fewer than 1% reach target doses
- No published implementation protocol provides operational guidance for primary care-led HF management in rural and resource-limited U.S. settings

## The Solution: HEARTLAND Protocol

An 8-module clinical implementation toolkit designed for primary care providers managing heart failure without specialist support, structured by the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR).

| Module | Focus |
|-|-|
| 1. Risk Stratification | CKM staging + HEARTLAND Risk Score (incorporates distance-to-care and social support — variables absent from MAGGIC, GWTG-HF, and SHFM) |
| 2. GDMT Optimization | Four-pillar therapy (ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) with Generic Bridge pathway (~$15/month) ensuring no patient untreated due to cost |
| 3. Telephone-Based Titration | Dual-track system: Digital (app-based) and Analog (voice telephone), validated by the Hozho Trial in rural Navajo Nation |
| 4. Discharge Transitions | Teach-back methodology, bedside medication delivery, structured 48-72h post-discharge contact |
| 5. Remote Monitoring | Basic kit ($50-150), "Human Filter" principle preventing inappropriate ED referrals, RPM/RTM billing codes for financial sustainability |
| 6. Comorbidity Management | Integrated guidance for AF, OSA, iron deficiency, diabetes, CKD, COPD, depression |
| 7. Primary Care Coordination | SBAR handoff tools, referral criteria, Shared Medical Appointments for capacity multiplication |
| 8. Implementation Guidance | Three-tier design accommodating Critical Access Hospitals (Tier 1) through regional centers (Tier 3) |

## Key Differentiators

- **Three-tier implementation**: Tier 1 (minimal resources) through Tier 3 (advanced) — incremental improvement is success
- **Dual-track monitoring**: Both digital and analog pathways follow identical clinical algorithms
- **Generic Bridge**: Ensures immediate GDMT access regardless of insurance or financial status
- **Rural-specific risk score**: First to incorporate distance-to-care and social isolation alongside clinical variables
- **Evidence transparency**: Every recommendation labeled as established, emerging, or pragmatic heuristic

## Evidence Foundation

- 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA HF Management Guideline
- Hozho Trial (telephone GDMT in rural Navajo Nation)
- TIM-HF2 (telemonitoring mortality reduction, greatest benefit in remote patients)
- CHAMP-HF Registry (GDMT implementation gap quantification)
- EMPEROR-Preserved and DELIVER trials (SGLT2i in HFpEF)
- FINEARTS-HF (finerenone in HFpEF) and STEP-HFpEF (semaglutide)
