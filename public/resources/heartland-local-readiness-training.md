# HEARTLAND local readiness and training pack

Educational preparation edition — 1 October 2026. Public educational edition.

**For synthetic tabletop preparation only. Not authorization for patient care or a pilot.**
This pack can be completed on paper or in an offline text editor. No application, account, internet
connection, automated message, or audio recording is needed to perform the exercises. Blank fields
are unresolved, not approved. All examples and exercise identifiers are synthetic.

## 1. Scope and use

Purpose: help a proposed implementation team demonstrate that it can identify responsibility,
track examinations and follow-up, distinguish incomplete steps, handle communication failures,
and reconcile paper records without inventing completion. It is an educational implementation-support
resource accompanying the proposed HEARTLAND framework, pending validation.

This document specifies administrative preparation, not clinical indications, thresholds, medication
doses, response-time rules, or a validated competency examination. An authorized clinician and the
institution must supply the applicable clinical and operational policies before clinical adoption.
No clinical emergency is assigned to a routine application queue by this pack. The institution's
approved emergency route must be explicit; absent that route, the readiness gate is unresolved.

The published Toolkit V3.4 §4.5 supplies the seven-stage evidence chain used below. This preparation
edition adds explicit pharmacy participation, context-sensitive referral and respectful escalation.
It is not an institution-specific training approval, professional credential or clinical acceptance
of a new deployment. A completed exercise never substitutes for those decisions.

### Allowed outcomes

- **Prepared for another synthetic rehearsal:** the listed exercise criteria were demonstrated;
  outstanding external adoption gates remain explicit.
- **Revise and repeat:** one or more observable criteria failed, were skipped, or were untested.
- **Stop:** real data, actual patient contact, unapproved external sending, an unidentified recipient,
  or another scope violation is encountered. Preserve permitted evidence and follow the designated
  exercise escalation route; do not continue by silently changing scope.

None of these outcomes means clinically approved, clinically validated, deployed, or ready for patients.

## 3. Role and authority map

Name people only when they have actually agreed to the role. The same person may hold more than one
role only if the institution permits it; record that explicitly. Never use “the AI”, a vendor, or
“the system” as the accountable clinical professional or backup.

|Role|Permitted responsibility to rehearse|Must not be inferred|Named person; acceptance evidence|
|-|-|-|-|
|Clinical approver|Adjudicates clinical policies, content and scope|Technical review is not clinical approval|________________|
|Ordering professional|Authorizes request and clinical plan within permitted scope|A draft is not an order|________________|
|Accountable review owner|Tracks open work and documents interpretation/action|Task existence or assignment is not acceptance or completed care|________________|
|Covering professional|Accepts the documented coverage interval and handoff|A name in a directory is not an agreed backup|________________|
|Coordinator / RN / MA / CHW, as locally permitted|Arranges access, tracks collection, supports education and routes gaps|Operational capability does not authorize prescribing or interpretation outside scope|________________|

|Communication professional|Documents recipient verification, explanation, understanding and follow-up|Message delivery is not understanding|________________|

**Recognition is not disposition.** Any appropriately trained team member may recognize and promptly
report a concern. Qualified staff assess it within scope. Medication changes and clinical disposition
require documented professional authority and applicable local agreements. Pharmacy support may be
local or remote at any tier; its availability is neither assumed nor a universal staffing prerequisite.

Before any operational adoption, explicitly answer:

1. Who reviews each class of unresolved work during and outside staffed hours?
2. Who accepts handover at a coverage change, absence or holiday?
3. What approved urgency policy applies? Where is the emergency route documented?
4. What happens when the primary, backup, communication route or application is unavailable?
5. Who detects a stopped process, missing collection, unread result or uncertain transport?
6. Who decides whether a later signal belongs to an existing or new episode?
7. How are the clinical response requirement, calendar/timezone and local capacity reconciled?

If a required answer is missing, mark the relevant gate unresolved. Do not fill it with a universal
laboratory-expiry interval, an application priority label, a daily scheduled job, or an invented SLA.

### Respectful escalation rehearsal

Reporter: “I am concerned about this finding or unfinished step. The source/version is ____. I need
the named decision-maker to review it under our policy. Who is accepting the next action?”

Recipient: “Thank you for raising this. I have understood the concern as ____. I accept ____;
the next action and timing under our policy are ____. If I become unavailable, the agreed backup is ____.”

Observer checks that concerns are acknowledged respectfully, politely and gratefully regardless of
role or seniority. An acknowledgment is not a clinical decision, and an unanswered message does not
transfer ownership. Use the accepted backup/emergency route when applicable; do not wait for a
routine software queue in an emergency. These phrases are rehearsal aids, not universal response rules.

Use “unknown” when the result is unknown. Keep these facts separate:

- Intent saved: work to notify was recorded; no external request is proven.
- Attempt started: a request may have reached a service; a timeout does not prove non-delivery.
- Service accepted: a technical response, not proof of patient receipt or comprehension.
- Contact confirmed: evidence that the intended person was reached, not necessarily that they understood.
- Understanding assessed: document what was asked/observed and remaining gaps; a played audio or quiz
  score alone is not professional teach-back or evidence of delivered care.

## 6. Downtime and recovery rehearsal

1. **Declare the exercise outage.** Record its scope and last confirmed source/packet identifiers.
   “Screen unavailable” is not “no pending work”. Use the agreed manual coverage route.
2. **Continue the authorized paper workflow.** Maintain owner, stage, source/version, actual times,
   attempted and confirmed contacts, and unresolved actions. Do not create fictitious results.
3. **Retain uncertain technical outcomes.** If a submission or send may have succeeded, record that
   uncertainty. Do not duplicate the examination or re-send merely because confirmation is absent.
4. **Restore only the authorized environment.** The technical operator verifies identity, current
   access and supported version. Do not reactivate an old transport path as an automatic fallback.
5. **Reconcile packet by packet.** Compare stable IDs, collection time, source/version and receipts;
   separate pre-existing records from missing ones. Use the supported recovery mechanism where one
   exists; otherwise keep the unresolved item assigned for authorized reconciliation.
6. **Check concurrent changes.** An owner, result version, contact or closure may have changed while
   offline. Preserve both observations and obtain the authorized resolution; no blind overwrite.
7. **Document the reconciliation result.** Record matched IDs, missing evidence, unresolved exceptions,
   reviewer and next action. Restored software does not itself close a clinical work item.

## 7. Facilitator training script

No live patient role is required. A participant can play the synthetic recipient and another the
covering professional. Do not dial, email, or message real recipients. Use printed response cards
or verbal simulation for all communications. Record actual session duration, not a promised standard.

|Block|Facilitator action|Learner demonstration|Record|
|-|-|-|-|
|Opening and boundaries|Read §1; inspect materials; identify held assets and stop conditions|Explain what this exercise cannot approve or prove|Participant restatement and gaps|
|Ownership and coverage|Provide an incomplete handover and an absent backup|Identify the still-responsible person, missing acceptance and contingency|Handover/coverage worksheet|
|Pharmacy and authority|Assign a community pharmacist at a fictional Tier 1 site; introduce a medication concern|Separate recognition, qualified assessment and authorized action; confirm accepted handoff without excluding pharmacy or inferring prescribing authority|Role map and concern/acceptance record|
|Seven-stage chain|Provide a request, a scheduling barrier and an unclassified result|Keep collection, result, review and communication separate|Completed paper packet|
|Exceptions and recovery|Introduce an uncertain save, corrected result and revoked access|Recover identity or keep uncertainty visible without duplicate or disclosure|Correction/reconciliation records|
|Communication and teaching|Play unanswered recipient, accepted technical delivery and unresolved understanding|Record attempts separately and keep remaining work assigned|Contact log|
|Assessment|Run the scenarios below without coaching the answer|Demonstrate every applicable observable criterion|Observer sheet and remediation|
|Debrief|Review discrepancies and agree on repeat exercises|Identify exact gaps, named owners and next review|Readiness decision, not clinical authorization|

For a short team huddle, cover three topics in order: (1) same clinically indicated goal, different
delivery resources; (2) who recognizes, assesses and authorizes, including pharmacy; (3) how the next
person accepts work and how the team knows it happened. Ask each participant to demonstrate a handoff,
not merely state that the slide or document was read. Preserve minimum safety preparation for every
team; a future study could compare that minimum with enhanced coaching, subject to separate approval.

The facilitator may explain material during training. During assessment, record prompting; a prompted
answer is not independent performance. Record “not tested” for skipped scenarios. Allow accessibility
accommodations and record them without changing the evidence requirement.

## 11. Publication status and remaining adoption gates

The 1 October release records identify Toolkit V3.4, App v1.10.0 and Pocket Guide v0.2.1 as published.
The App runtime also includes later citation/footer/test corrections. That status is distinct from
institutional deployment or patient use. This pack does not remove the following gates:

- Urgency, out-of-hours responsibility and post-closure recurrence require actual clinical policies.
  Application priority labels are not validated clinical response guarantees.
- Coverage requires named human primary/backup and an accepted contingency, not a template alone.
- Separate ambulatory entry remains deferred. ESSI instrument/version/scoring adjudication remains open.
- Use the current card library and each card's own prototype/distribution limitations. Historical
  card versions are not silently substituted as newly approved training material.
- The public sandbox release contains 58 synthetic clips (31 English, 27 Spanish). Automated
  transcription and technical checks are not human listening or pronunciation certification.
  Playback release does not authorize real calls, clinical use or assumptions of patient understanding.
  Scenario S10 concerns a hypothetical unverified recording, not a blanket hold on those 58 clips.
- Deployed work records distinguish request, collection, result/version, review, communication and
  evidenced closure. Technical verification does not prove that a team delivered those steps to patients.
  External notification dispatch and institution-gated background processes remain disabled.
- Recipient/patient erasure, retention, vendor/institutional approval and live operational verification
  remain separate adoption requirements. A public sandbox is synthetic, not evidence of patient care.
- Patient pilot work remains outside the requested execution. No recruitment, patient data or outcomes
  are produced by these materials.
